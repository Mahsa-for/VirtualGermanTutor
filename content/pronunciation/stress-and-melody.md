# German Word Stress and Sentence Melody (Pronunciation)

## Word stress

- Native German words are usually stressed on the **first syllable**:
  *ARbeit, DANke, MUtter, LEHrerin*.
- **Inseparable prefixes** (be-, ge-, er-, ver-, ent-, emp-, zer-) are **unstressed**:
  *verGESsen, beKOMmen, erKLÄren*.
- **Separable prefixes** (an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-) are **stressed**:
  *AUFstehen, EINkaufen, MITkommen*.
- Loan words often keep end stress: *StuDENT, RestauRANT, TeleFON*.

## The "Knacklaut" (glottal stop)

German separates vowel-initial words with a tiny stop, unlike the English liaison:
*das Auto* = "das ʔauto" (not "dasauto"); *am Abend* = "am ʔabend".

## Sentence melody (intonation)

- **Statements** and **W-questions** fall at the end:
  *Ich komme aus Berlin.↘*  *Woher kommst du?↘*
- **Yes/no questions** rise at the end: *Kommst du mit?↗*
- Lists rise on each item and fall on the last:
  *Ich kaufe Brot,↗ Käse↗ und Milch.↘*

## Common mistakes and corrections

- ❌ stressing the prefix in *verstehen* → ✅ *ver-STE-hen*.
- ❌ English sing-song rising intonation on statements → ✅ let statements fall.
- Separable verbs split in main clauses: *Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**.* (stress stays on AUF).
