# German Vowels and Umlauts (Pronunciation)

## Long vs short vowels

- A vowel is **long** before a single consonant or when doubled, or followed by *h*:
  *Tag, Saat, Bahn*.
- A vowel is **short** before a double consonant or a cluster: *Mann, kommen, Stadt*.

## The umlauts ä, ö, ü

These do not exist in English – practice them carefully.

- **ä** ≈ the "e" in *bed* (short) or a long open "e": *Mädchen, spät, Käse*.
- **ö** – round your lips as for "o" but say "e". Like French *eu* in *peu*:
  *schön, hören, möchte, Köln*.
- **ü** – round your lips as for "u" but say "ee". Like French *u* in *tu*:
  *über, Tür, fünf, müde, für*.

Minimal pairs to drill:
- *schon* (already) vs *schön* (beautiful)
- *Kuchen* (cake) vs *Küche* (kitchen)
- *Mutter* (mother) vs *Mütter* (mothers)

## Diphthongs

- **ei / ai** = "eye": *mein, Wein, Mai*.
- **ie** = long "ee": *die, Liebe, Bier*.  ⚠️ *ei* and *ie* are opposites!
- **au** = "ow" as in *house*: *Haus, Frau, laufen*.
- **eu / äu** = "oy" as in *boy*: *heute, neun, Häuser*.

## Common mistakes and corrections

- Saying *ü* like English "oo": *für* is **not** "foor" – lips rounded, tongue forward.
- Mixing *ei* and *ie*: *Liebe* = "LEE-be", *Bein* = "bine".
- The final **-e** is always pronounced (a soft "uh"): *Katze* = "KAT-ze", not "kats".
