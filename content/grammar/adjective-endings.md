# German Adjective Endings (A2–B1)

Adjectives before a noun take an ending that depends on the article type, gender,
and case. There are three declension patterns.

## 1. After a definite article (der/die/das) – "weak" endings

Mostly **-e** or **-en**.

| Case | Masc | Fem | Neut | Plural |
|------|------|-----|------|--------|
| Nom | der gut**e** Mann | die gut**e** Frau | das gut**e** Kind | die gut**en** Kinder |
| Akk | den gut**en** Mann | die gut**e** Frau | das gut**e** Kind | die gut**en** Kinder |
| Dat | dem gut**en** Mann | der gut**en** Frau | dem gut**en** Kind | den gut**en** Kindern |

## 2. After an indefinite article (ein/kein/mein) – "mixed" endings

The adjective shows the gender where *ein* can't:
- *ein gut**er** Mann*, *eine gut**e** Frau*, *ein gut**es** Kind*
- *einen gut**en** Mann*, *mit einem gut**en** Freund*

## 3. No article – "strong" endings (adjective carries the case signal)

- *gut**er** Wein*, *gut**e** Milch*, *gut**es** Brot*, *gut**e** Äpfel*
- *mit gut**em** Wein*, *bei schön**em** Wetter*

## Rule of thumb

> If the article already shows the case/gender clearly, the adjective ending is
> weak (-e/-en). If there is no article, the adjective must do that work (strong ending).

## Common mistakes and corrections

- ❌ *ein gute Mann* → ✅ *ein gut**er** Mann*
- ❌ *mit dem gute Freund* → ✅ *mit dem gut**en** Freund*
- ❌ *Ich trinke kalt Wasser.* → ✅ *Ich trinke kalt**es** Wasser.*
